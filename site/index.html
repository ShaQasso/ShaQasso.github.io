Research

Gut bacteria can change what they do without changing what they are.

During my Ph.D. in Naama Geva-Zatorsky's lab at the Technion, I studied how gut bacteria switch their function within a species, what triggers those switches, and what they mean for the host.

Phase variation

Inflammation, phages and diet move bacterial DNA switches

Bacteroides can reverse segments of their own DNA, which switches genes such as those that make the capsule on or off. We showed that inflammation and bacteriophages shift these inversion states in the gut, and that the shift changes how the bacteria affect the immune system. A follow-up showed that phage-driven inversions shape B. fragilis function over the long term. The same switches also respond to diet: in B. thetaiotaomicron, different carbohydrates changed inversion states and immune-modulatory functions, in work led by Noa Gal-Mandelbaum.

Chronotype

The gut microbiome of morning people and night owls

In a collaboration with the Tauber and Shochat labs, a metagenomic analysis of people with different chronotypes found a gut microbiome signature associated with being a morning or an evening person. The work was covered by phys.org and the Technion.

  • Metagenomic analysis reveals the signature of gut microbiota associated with human chronotypes FASEB Journal, 2021, first author
  • Press · phys.org: Microbiome differences between night owls and morning people
  • Press · Technion: Does diet dictate sleep? (also in Hebrew)
Clinical microbiomes

Microbiome questions from the clinic

Alongside the main project, I worked on capsule fecal transplantation to clear resistant Enterobacteriaceae, followed up in a mouse carriage model, and on microbiome patterns in Lynch syndrome, leaks after esophagectomy and infected diabetic foot ulcers.

  • Oral capsulized fecal microbiota transplantation for eradication of carbapenemase-producing Enterobacteriaceae colonization Clinical Infectious Diseases, 2020, co-first author with Haggai Bar-Yoseph
  • Microbiome changes associated with FMT-mediated clearance of antibiotic-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae in a murine carriage model BMC Microbiology, 2026, first author
  • Gut microbial signatures are associated with Lynch syndrome and cancer history in Druze communities in Israel Scientific Reports, 2023, co-first author with Rawi Naddaf
  • Esophageal microbiome correlates with post-esophagectomy anastomotic leak in cancer patients ESMO Gastrointestinal Oncology, 2025, co-first author with Rawi Naddaf and Haim Shmilovich
  • Microbiome characterization of infected diabetic foot ulcers in association with clinical outcomes Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology, 2022, co-first author with Hadar Mudrik-Zohar
